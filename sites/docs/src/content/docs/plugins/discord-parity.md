---
title: Discord and simulator parity
description: Two-account observations on the Cicord community server, the corresponding simulator checks and the limits of the comparison.
---

On 26 November 2026, a server owner and a consenting ordinary member tested the deployed Cicord 0.1.11 bot on the official community server. The 0.2.0 release candidate ran in local and CI simulators. The live observations below describe what each signed-in participant actually saw. Production plugin versions and settings determine which workflows can be compared.

| Scenario | Observed on Discord | Candidate regression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Join and self-assigned roles | A welcome mention and Member role appeared. The member removed their Plugin author role using a button, received a private confirmation, then restored it. The moderator log recorded both changes. | The candidate reaction-roles scenario exercises eligibility, role removal and restoration, and access to a private channel before and after the role change. |
| Staff-only channels and commands | The owner saw the staff channels and the server moderation timeout command. The ordinary member could see the public help channels. The staff channels and the moderation command were absent from their picker. | `TestSimulatorActorViews` and `TestCommandPickerUsesTheInvokingMembersPermissions` check channel visibility, history and available commands under each identity. The operator can still inspect the whole simulated guild. |
| Private ticket | The member opened a ticket. Each participant saw the other’s reply. The member closed the ticket, its channel disappeared, and staff received a summary naming both participants and counting three human messages. | The tickets CLI suite covers the opener, staff and an unrelated member, revocation during a conversation, and private attachments. These three-way access and reconnect cases also use synthetic identities. |
| Timeout, DM and recovery | The owner requested a one-minute timeout. The member saw the countdown and the communication restriction, and read the reason and duration in a bot DM. The ordinary composer returned without a moderator action. A new member message was delivered. Staff could read the case log. | `TestMemberTimeoutAcrossTicketDMAndVoice` uses a controlled clock and checks history, DM delivery and restricted actions before and after expiry. Discord also documents a separate rule for using an existing reaction during a timeout. [Discord timeout FAQ](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/4413305239191-Time-Out-FAQ) |
| Bot-managed poll | The plugin rejected an unconfigured destination. The owner opened an A/B poll in an allowed channel. The member voted A, received a refusal when attempting to close it, and saw the owner close it with one vote for A. | The polls simulator drives a non-author and an author, checks the final choices and verifies anonymous portal results. |
| Native poll | The member voted B, withdrew it, then voted A. The count went from one to zero to one. The owner saw 100% for A and 0% for B, ended the poll and saw Discord declare A the winner. | The candidate CLI drives the same sequence on a poll created by the plugin. It compares simulator message counts, portal aggregates and both versions of the closed event. Live Cicord used its button-based plugin. The native live observation covers Discord’s client and tally. |
| Voice room | Joining the hub created a room and moved the owner into it. Locking denied the guest. After an explicit permit, both muted participants appeared in the same room. The room survived the owner’s departure, then disappeared after the guest disconnected. Native audit entries recorded creation, move and deletion by the bot. | The candidate voice-rooms suite checks lock, permit, transfer, departure order and cleanup. `TestVoiceRoomLockPermitAndOwnerMovement` checks visibility and connection rights separately. The live hub’s configured role restriction also prevented the guest from claiming the room. |
| Audit evidence | The moderator read the timeout target, actor, QA reason and expiry in Discord’s native audit log. Two synthetic messages from the same member were individually deleted by the same moderator about 2.2 seconds apart. The native audit log showed one entry with a count of two, actor, author and channel. It was visible within 15 seconds of the second confirmation, including navigation and loading. Discord displayed no per-message identifier in that row. | The host’s attribution tests require independent actor, target and operation evidence. The simulator also exercises missing, delayed and merged audit entries. |

## Interpreting the two views

Select the participant in the Simulator panel, or pass `userId` to the state and message-history endpoints. Restrict the command query by member, guild and channel to reproduce the command picker. The member view refreshes after a role or channel overwrite changes. To exercise native voting, post to `/api/v1/sim/poll-votes`, read the poll message from each member’s view, and compare the final counts with the plugin route. [Simulator endpoints](../../reference/http-api/simulator/)

Two signed-in accounts can reproduce an owner/member interaction. A negative ticket check against an unrelated third member, different simultaneous moderator identities, and DM routing across two configured guilds need additional identities or guilds. The registry tests exercise these with isolated fixtures. The verification plugin also exercised both vendors’ public test-mode Siteverify endpoints. Passing and failing challenges produced the corresponding role outcomes in the simulated guild. [Turnstile testing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/troubleshooting/testing/) · [hCaptcha test keys](https://docs.hcaptcha.com/#integration-testing-test-keys) The candidate’s verification, guard, appeals, giveaways, transfers, rewards, suggestions, starboard, staff shifts and scheduled events also run against synthetic members. A successful fixture establishes the candidate behavior on that backend. Live gateway timing, Discord rate limits, a production CAPTCHA flow and OAuth recovery after a ban each require their own observation.

The first anti-nuke delivery runs in monitor mode. Enabling automatic enforcement requires timing evidence from multiple active moderators and real audit entries. The branch does not make a production latency claim from the two-account checks.
